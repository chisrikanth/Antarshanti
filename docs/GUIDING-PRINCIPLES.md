# Guiding principles for everything on this site

## 1. Lower people's pressure; never add to it or cause fear
Anything a visitor reads or does here should leave them feeling lighter and more supported than when they arrived. No fear, no urgency, no guilt, no "what are you waiting for", no scary numbers.

## 2. Tell the truth, not a rosy story
Do not cover real difficulty with cheerful words. If someone's answers show they are struggling, say so plainly and kindly, and point to real help. Calm, honest and warm beats comforting and untrue.

## 3. Nothing we cannot stand behind
Complementary support, not cures. "Many people find", "studies report", "may help". No promised outcomes, no health claims we cannot support, no invented numbers or testimonials.

## 4. Start with what is good
Show strengths and what is going well first, then what could use care. Use ordinary words, not labels. Colours help people see the difference at a glance.

## 5. A human is always one tap away
"Talk to Srikanth on WhatsApp" is available at any point, at the top and the bottom. Safety information (Tele-MANAS 14416, a doctor or counsellor) is never hidden and never salesy. No programme is promoted to someone who may be in acute distress.

## 6. Respect people's data
Ask only for what is needed, with consent. Be clear what is stored and why. Test data never mixes with real data.

## How these were applied (Nov 2026)
- Check-in: questions start with positives, hard questions sit in the middle, it ends on a positive area. Results show "Your inner resources" before "How you have been feeling". No score out of 100; no zero bars. Wording is plain ("Doing well", "A little now and then", "Showing up often", "Heavy right now").
- Domain names softened: "Worry and tension", "Feeling low", "Emotional balance".
- Pressure lines removed: "Don't settle for happy-ish", "why haven't you tried it yet?".

## Checklist before publishing any new text
1. Would a worried, tired person feel calmer after reading this?
2. Is every statement true and something we can support?
3. Is there a gentle way to say it that does not frighten?
4. Is the human option (WhatsApp) and the safety information visible?
